Party & current role

Rhoden: Republican. Governor since Jan. 2025 (succeeded Kristi Noem when she left to lead DHS). Running mate: Lt. Gov. Tony Venhuizen. Won GOP nomination in SD's first statewide runoff, July 28, 2026, over Toby Doeden [1][26].

Ahlers: Democrat. SD Democratic Party executive director since 2023 (on leave for the campaign). Running mate: Steven McCleerey, Sisseton farmer and former legislator [14][26].

Background / career

Rhoden: Born Sturgis, 1959. Union Center rancher since 1981; ranch-supply foreman 1980–91. National Guard 1978–85. SD House 2001–08 (majority leader) and 2017–18; SD Senate 2009–14 (majority whip); Lt. Gov. 2019–25. Lost 2014 GOP U.S. Senate primary to Rounds [1][2].

Ahlers: Born Dell Rapids; B.A. Augustana, 1997. Small-business owner (video stores; The Jabberwock coffee shop); substitute teacher; led Dell Rapids Chamber. Three legislative terms (House and Senate; Joint Appropriations). 2020 Dem. U.S. Senate nominee vs. Rounds; lost 2022 District 25 House race. Age 52 [13][14].

Economy / taxes

Rhoden: Signed 2026 property tax package he calls "the largest property tax cut in the history of the state" (sales-tax redirect, $56M general funds, optional 0.5% county sales tax); says homeowner taxes fall ~36% on average. Cites Future Fund guardrails and recruited plants [3].

Ahlers: Calls Rhoden's plan "a tax shift" from property to sales tax; wants a "comprehensive" tax-structure review, more relief for seniors, veterans, and people with disabilities; incentives with transparency and help for "mom and pop" businesses [3][16].

Medicaid / health care

Rhoden: Defended Amendment I at the Sept. 5 debate, citing federal debt. Said at the debate: "I voted against it because I kept saying, 'How long are the feds going to stay at 90%?'" (the source does not say which vote) [4][27]

Ahlers: Opposes Amendment I; called it "a cop out." Keep rural hospitals open; mental-health access [4][15][27].

Military & veterans

Rhoden: Served in the National Guard 1978–85. Endorsed by Veterans for America First [1]. Specific veterans policy: Not found.◐ Thin evidenceVeterans for America First endorsement: Single reference source (Ballotpedia list). No date given.

Ahlers: Military service: none found. Wants more property tax relief for veterans (among others) [3].

AI / tech

Rhoden: Signed law making large (10 MW+) data centers cover their power costs, protecting local regulation, requiring water-use review [3].

Ahlers: More local control over data centers; rural broadband; favors small business over hyperscale data centers [3][15][29].◐ Thin evidenceData-center views: Mostly the campaign's own site plus one radio summary (KXLG). No detailed plan found.

Agriculture

Rhoden: Rancher. Vetoed lab-grown meat ban (Feb. 9, 2026), proposing a 5-yr moratorium; House sustained the veto 32–32 on Mar. 30, 2026 (47 votes needed) [34]. Signed 2025 ban on eminent domain for CO2 pipelines. On Trump's tariff-free beef imports: "I thought he was wrongheaded" [4][10][24].

Ahlers: Family farms and Main Street investment; opposes tariff-free foreign beef imports; opposes private companies' use of eminent domain [4][15][16].

Other notable

Rhoden: Public safety and Operation Prairie Thunder (Highway Patrol/ICE collaboration); water-infrastructure task force; $650M Sioux Falls prison approved Sept. 2025 [12][22][26].

Ahlers: Free school lunches for all students; child-care tax credit/rebate (details not given); tribal relations commission with all nine tribes; on ICE: "I don't like officers wearing masks" [3][14][16].
