About this guide

Nonpartisan. No endorsement or recommendation. Every candidate gets the same rows and the same sourcing standard.

Compiled from public records (FEC, SD Secretary of State, SD Legislature, U.S. Senate and House filings) and news coverage. Bracketed numbers link to that race's sources. “Not found” means not found in the sources checked. Things can change before Election Day.

Evidence labels: no label = verified (primary source or 2+ credible outlets). ◐ Thin evidence = one secondary source, headline only, partisan poll, campaign self-report or stale data. ! Unverified = not confirmed in any source checked.

Governor

Larry Rhoden (R, incumbent) vs. Dan Ahlers (D). The Democratic nominee is Dan Ahlers (Toby Doeden was Rhoden's GOP runoff opponent). State money figures are older than federal ones: SD required no pre-runoff report, and pre-general reports are due Oct. 19.

Larry Rhoden (Republican)
Governor (incumbent)
Union Center rancher; governor since Jan. 2025; former Lt. Gov. and legislative leader; National Guard 1978–85.

Dan Ahlers (Democrat)

Party & current role

Rhoden: Republican. Governor since Jan. 2025 (succeeded Kristi Noem when she left to lead DHS). Running mate: Lt. Gov. Tony Venhuizen. Won GOP nomination in SD's first statewide runoff, July 28, 2026, over Toby Doeden [1][26].

Ahlers: Democrat. SD Democratic Party executive director since 2023 (on leave for the campaign). Running mate: Steven McCleerey, Sisseton farmer and former legislator [14][26].

Background / career

Rhoden: Born Sturgis, 1959. Union Center rancher since 1981; ranch-supply foreman 1980–91. National Guard 1978–85. SD House 2001–08 (majority leader) and 2017–18; SD Senate 2009–14 (majority whip); Lt. Gov. 2019–25. Lost 2014 GOP U.S. Senate primary to Rounds [1][2].

Ahlers: Born Dell Rapids; B.A. Augustana, 1997. Small-business owner (video stores; The Jabberwock coffee shop); substitute teacher; led Dell Rapids Chamber. Three legislative terms (House and Senate; Joint Appropriations). 2020 Dem. U.S. Senate nominee vs. Rounds; lost 2022 District 25 House race. Age 52 [13][14].

Economy / taxes

Rhoden: Signed 2026 property tax package he calls "the largest property tax cut in the history of the state" (sales-tax redirect, $56M general funds, optional 0.5% county sales tax); says homeowner taxes fall ~36% on average. Cites Future Fund guardrails and recruited plants [3].

Ahlers: Calls Rhoden's plan "a tax shift" from property to sales tax; wants a "comprehensive" tax-structure review, more relief for seniors, veterans, and people with disabilities; incentives with transparency and help for "mom and pop" businesses [3][16].

Medicaid / health care

Rhoden: Defended Amendment I at the Sept. 5 debate, citing federal debt. Said at the debate: "I voted against it because I kept saying, 'How long are the feds going to stay at 90%?'" (the source does not say which vote) [4][27]

Ahlers: Opposes Amendment I; called it "a cop out." Keep rural hospitals open; mental-health access [4][15][27].

Military & veterans

Rhoden: Served in the National Guard 1978–85. Endorsed by Veterans for America First [1]. Specific veterans policy: Not found.◐ Thin evidenceVeterans for America First endorsement: Single reference source (Ballotpedia list). No date given.

Ahlers: Military service: none found. Wants more property tax relief for veterans (among others) [3].

AI / tech

Rhoden: Signed law making large (10 MW+) data centers cover their power costs, protecting local regulation, requiring water-use review [3].

Ahlers: More local control over data centers; rural broadband; favors small business over hyperscale data centers [3][15][29].◐ Thin evidenceData-center views: Mostly the campaign's own site plus one radio summary (KXLG). No detailed plan found.

Agriculture

Rhoden: Rancher. Vetoed lab-grown meat ban (Feb. 9, 2026), proposing a 5-yr moratorium; House sustained the veto 32–32 on Mar. 30, 2026 (47 votes needed) [34]. Signed 2025 ban on eminent domain for CO2 pipelines. On Trump's tariff-free beef imports: "I thought he was wrongheaded" [4][10][24].

Ahlers: Family farms and Main Street investment; opposes tariff-free foreign beef imports; opposes private companies' use of eminent domain [4][15][16].

Other notable

Rhoden: Public safety and Operation Prairie Thunder (Highway Patrol/ICE collaboration); water-infrastructure task force; $650M Sioux Falls prison approved Sept. 2025 [12][22][26].

Ahlers: Free school lunches for all students; child-care tax credit/rebate (details not given); tribal relations commission with all nine tribes; on ICE: "I don't like officers wearing masks" [3][14][16].

Notable record / actions

Rhoden: Documented: property tax and data center laws; lab-meat veto; CO2 eminent-domain ban; signed the FY2026 budget (Mar. 31, 2025) that restored SD Public Broadcasting funding [35][36]; concealed-carry expansions [3][10][24].◐ Thin evidenceConcealed-carry expansions: Single source, an opinion column (Mar. 26, 2025), cited for facts only.

Ahlers: Documented: sponsored law creating advisory committee on language development for deaf/hard-of-hearing children (signed by Gov. Daugaard) [14].

Controversies / reversals (documented)

Rhoden: Documented omission: private-plane travel from a benefactor was left off his 2025 year-end report; campaign called it "inadvertent" [5]. His amended year-end report (filed Apr. 27, 2026) adds in-kind "Airplane Transportation" of $657.34 from Riddle's Group, Inc., and lifts donated goods from $5,509.50 to $10,068.95 [30][31]. Documented: T. Denny Sanford gave $50K to the federally registered Free American Fund, which (with other donors' money) transferred $85,500 to Rhoden's state committee. SD's $4K individual limit does not cap PAC transfers, and an OpenSecrets analyst said he saw nothing illegal [6]. Reversal: backed $825M Lincoln County prison, then reset after legislative loss (Feb. 2025) [11]. At the Sept. 28 debate he said "We've never been criticized one bit on the plane usage." The News Watch fact brief found no public criticism of his state-plane use, but noted the March criticism over undisclosed private-plane travel [5].

Ahlers: Disputed claim: said The Dakota Scout "had to sue the state less than a year ago to get state records"; Rhoden called it "patently false." The News Watch fact brief did not rule on this claim. The only related source it lists is a Scout story on the state backing off a legal threat over Noem credit-card records [5]. Context: 2023 resigned as party director alleging a hostile work environment by then-chair (allegation by Ahlers); rehired later in 2023 [18]. Documented reversals: none found.! UnverifiedAhlers' "had to sue the state" claim: Ahlers' claim that The Dakota Scout sued for state records was not confirmed or refuted by the fact brief or any source checked.◐ Thin evidence2023 hostile-work-environment allegation: Ahlers' own allegation, reported by one outlet (Dakota News Now, 2023). No finding.

Constitutional Amendments I–L

Summaries from the Attorney General's ballot explanations and news coverage. Read the official ballot wording before voting.

Amendment I: Medicaid expansion trigger

A “Yes” vote: Adds a condition: the expansion requirement applies only while the federal share stays at 90% or more. Disputed effect: AG Jackley's explanation says expansion ends automatically below 90%; sponsor Rep. Mortenson (R) and Lt. Gov. Venhuizen say the decision goes back to lawmakers [3][4].◐ Thin evidenceEffect is disputed: The AG's official explanation and the legislative sponsors disagree on whether expansion ends automatically. Both views are on record; no court ruling.

A “No” vote: Expanded Medicaid stays in the constitution without this trigger [1][2].

Candidates: Rhoden defended it; Ahlers opposes ("a cop out"); Jackley wrote ballot explanation, no personal position; Rounds, Bengs, Gronli: Not found [5].

Amendment J: Citizenship and voting

A “Yes” vote: States in the constitution that noncitizens cannot vote [1][2].

A “No” vote: Constitution's voting language unchanged [1][2].

Candidates: No candidate position found (Jackley: AG explanation only).

Amendment K: Unclaimed-property trust fund

A “Yes” vote: Creates a constitutional trust fund for unclaimed-property proceeds; part of its earnings go to the general fund [1][2].

A “No” vote: No constitutional trust fund created [1][2].

Candidates: No candidate position found (Jackley: AG explanation only).

Also: Oct. 15 Searchlight explainer on the unclaimed-property trust fund (no candidate quotes).

Amendment L: 60% threshold for amendments

A “Yes” vote: Future constitutional amendments would need 60% voter approval to pass [1][2][6].◐ Thin evidenceSpending figure: ~$260K Fairness Project figure is from one outlet's reading of SOS reports (SD Searchlight, Oct. 2).

A “No” vote: Current approval threshold for amendments stays as it is [1][2].

Candidates: No candidate position found. Fairness Project ~$260K opposing so far (Oct. 14) [6][7].
